# Intermediate finite-schedule lower bound: area at least 7/100

Date: 2026-08-03

**Status:** superseded as the current headline by the exact variational theorem
`FINITE-SCHEDULE-VARIATIONAL.md`.  The 32-segment schedule in
`UNIVERSAL-141-OVER-2000.md` certifies `C_FS >= 141/2000`.  This note remains an
independently certified intermediate theorem.

## Theorem

Every star-shaped Kakeya set `E` satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L^{2*}(E)\ge\frac7{100}=0.07.}
\]

No measurability, continuity, boundedness, or selector regularity is assumed.
The proof uses the arbitrary-selector radial-sliver theorem from `THEORY.md`, a
finite outer-measure covering lemma, and pairwise-disjoint radial schedules.

The improvement over the previous bound is exact:

\[
\frac7{100}-\frac{1717}{25000}=\frac{33}{25000}>0.
\]

## 1. Radial weak-type input

Choose one unit needle in every projective direction and let `rho` be the radial
function of their star hull.  Put

\[
q(u)=\bigl|\{\phi\in S^1:\rho(\phi)>u\}\bigr|^*.
\]

For a selected needle write

\[
m_\theta=\min_{x\in N_\theta}|x|,
\qquad
M_\theta=\max_{x\in N_\theta}|x|,
\qquad
h_\theta=\operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{aff}N_\theta).
\]

Theorem A states that for \(0<r<R\) and \(r'<r\),

\[
q(r')\ge
\bigl|\{\theta:m_\theta\le r,\ M_\theta\ge R\}\bigr|^*
\frac{R-r}{R+r}. \tag{1}
\]

Since `q` is nonincreasing, letting \(r'\uparrow r\) gives the same inequality
for the left limit of `q`.  A monotone function differs from its left-limit
version only at countably many points, so (1) can be integrated in `r` without
changing any radial integral.

The exact chord geometry also gives

\[
M_\theta^2\ge m_\theta^2+\frac14. \tag{2}
\]

Finally, the origin triangle of one needle has area \(h_\theta/2\).

## 2. Finite outer-measure covering lemma

The only new measure-theoretic ingredient is finite; no continuum
outer-Fubini or selector pushforward is used.

> **Lemma F (finite fractional cover for Lebesgue outer measure).**
> Let \(X\) be a subset of the projective circle, let
> \(B_1,\ldots,B_N\subset X\) be arbitrary sets, and let
> \(d_1,\ldots,d_N\ge0\).  If
> \[
> p(\theta):=\sum_{i=1}^N d_i\mathbf1_{B_i}(\theta)\ge c
> \quad\text{for every }\theta\in X,
> \]
> then
> \[
> \sum_{i=1}^N d_i|B_i|^*\ge c|X|^*. \tag{3}
> \]

**Proof.** Lebesgue outer measure is strongly submodular.  Indeed, choose
measurable hulls \(H_A\supset A\), \(H_B\supset B\) with
\(|H_A|=|A|^*\), \(|H_B|=|B|^*\).  Then

\[
|A\cup B|^*+|A\cap B|^*
\le |H_A\cup H_B|+|H_A\cap H_B|
=|A|^*+|B|^*.
\]

Restrict outer measure to the finite Boolean algebra generated by
\(B_1,\ldots,B_N\).  Identify its finitely many atoms with a finite ground
set and define the set function \(v(J)\) as the outer measure of the union of
the atoms indexed by \(J\).  The displayed strong-submodularity inequality
says exactly that \(v\) is submodular.  The finite Choquet integral is then the
Lovasz extension of \(v\); the usual level-set decomposition shows that this
extension is convex and positively homogeneous, hence subadditive.  Extending
\(p\) by zero outside \(X\), we obtain

\[
\int^C p\,d|\cdot|^*
\le\sum_i d_i|B_i|^*.
\]

On the other hand, monotonicity and \(p\ge c\mathbf1_X\) give

\[
\int^C p\,d|\cdot|^*\ge c|X|^*.
\]

Combining proves (3).  No set \(B_i\) is assumed measurable. \(\square\)

This lemma is deliberately finite.  A corresponding assertion for a completely
arbitrary continuum family can fail for nonmeasurable sections and is not used
below.

## 3. Height split and direction classes

Set

\[
\eta=\frac7{50}.
\]

If \(\sup_\theta h_\theta\ge\eta\), including the infinite or non-attained
supremum cases, the single-triangle argument gives

\[
\mathcal L^{2*}(E)\ge\frac\eta2=\frac7{100}. \tag{4}
\]

Assume henceforth that every \(h_\theta<\eta\).

For \(j=0,\ldots,7\), put

\[
a_j=\frac{7j}{400},
\qquad
b_j=\frac{7(j+1)}{400},
\]

and define

\[
L_0=\{\theta:0\le m_\theta\le b_0\},
\qquad
L_j=\{\theta:a_j<m_\theta\le b_j\}\quad(j\ge1).
\]

Let \(L=\bigcup_{j=0}^7L_j=\{m\le\eta\}\).

For \(k\ge0\), define the high classes

\[
A_k=\eta+\frac{k}{2},
\qquad
B_k=A_k+\frac12,
\qquad
S_k=\{\theta:A_k<m_\theta\le B_k\}. \tag{5}
\]

These arbitrary sets form a pointwise disjoint countable partition of the
projective direction circle.  Outer-measure subadditivity gives

\[
\pi\le |L|^*+\sum_{k\ge0}|S_k|^*. \tag{6}
\]

## 4. Finite low-radius schedule

Use the rational window radii

\[
\begin{aligned}
(R_0,\ldots,R_7)=(&1/2,\ 5003/10000,\ 1253/2500,\ 5027/10000,\\
                  &631/1250,\ 203/400,\ 5109/10000,\ 5147/10000),
\end{aligned}
\]

on the consecutive radial intervals below, taken half-open on the right except
for the last endpoint (the endpoint convention is immaterial to integration),

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
0 &[7/400,\ 671/2000]\\
1 &[671/2000,\ 3419/10000]\\
2 &[3419/10000,\ 44/125]\\
3 &[44/125,\ 3661/10000]\\
4 &[3661/10000,\ 3841/10000]\\
5 &[3841/10000,\ 163/400]\\
6 &[163/400,\ 1098/2500]\\
7 &[1098/2500,\ 5147/10000].
\end{array} \tag{7}
\]

At every radial level exactly one window is active, so this schedule never
charges the same radial area twice.

For \(j\ge1\), direct rational arithmetic gives

\[
R_j^2\le\frac14+a_j^2.
\]

Consequently, if \(\theta\in L_j\), then (2) implies
\(M_\theta>R_j\), and hence \(M_\theta>R_i\) for every \(i\le j\).
For \(L_0\), (2) gives \(M_\theta\ge1/2=R_0\).

Let \(I_i\) denote the half-open radial interval in row \(i\) of (7), and
define

\[
\operatorname{LowPay}:=\sum_{i=0}^7\int_{I_i}u\,q(u)\,du.
\]

Within each interval in (7), discard partially eligible classes and retain only
those complete classes for which both \(m_\theta\le u\) and
\(M_\theta\ge R_i\) are already forced.  The retained direction union changes
only at the finitely many numbers \(b_j\) and the endpoints in (7).  On each
resulting radial cell, (1) first gives

\[
q(u-)\ge |C_\ell|^*\frac{R_i-u}{R_i+u}.
\]

Since \(q(u-)=q(u)\) almost everywhere, radial integration gives a finite sum

\[
\operatorname{LowPay}\ge\sum_{\ell=1}^N d_\ell|C_\ell|^*, \tag{8}
\]

where every \(C_\ell\) is a finite union of the arbitrary classes \(L_j\), and
\(d_\ell\) is the exact integral of
\(u(R_i-u)/(R_i+u)\) on the corresponding radial cell.

A direction in \(L_j\) receives at least

\[
P_j=
\int_{b_j}^{671/2000}
 u\frac{R_0-u}{R_0+u}\,du
+
\sum_{i=1}^{j}
\int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}
 u\frac{R_i-u}{R_i+u}\,du, \tag{9}
\]

where \((t_1,\ldots,t_8)\) are the last eight endpoints in (7).  Empty endpoint
pieces are interpreted as zero.

The exact certificate proves, for every \(j=0,\ldots,7\),

\[
P_j>c_*:=\frac{371}{16650}. \tag{10}
\]

Apply Lemma F to the finite simple payment function represented by (8).  Since
that function is at least \(P_j>c_*\) on every \(L_j\),

\[
\operatorname{LowPay}\ge c_*|L|^*. \tag{11}
\]

This is the step that replaces an invalid continuum pushforward argument.

## 5. High-radius ledgers

For \(a\ge\eta\), put

\[
R(a)=\sqrt{a^2+1+2\sqrt{a^2-\eta^2}}.
\]

Because \(h_\theta<\eta<m_\theta\) on \(S_k\), the perpendicular foot is
outside the segment and exact chord geometry gives

\[
M_\theta>R(A_k)
\qquad(\theta\in S_k). \tag{12}
\]

Assign \(S_k\) the radial ledger

\[
K_k=[B_k,R(A_k)].
\]

These ledgers are nonempty and pairwise disjoint: the function
\(D(a)=R(a)-a\) is increasing, while \(R(a)<a+1=B_{k+1}\).
They are also disjoint from the low schedule, because the low schedule ends at
\(5147/10000\) and \(K_0\) starts at

\[
B_0=\eta+\frac12=\frac{16}{25}.
\]

Theorem A gives

\[
\operatorname{HighPay}
\ge\sum_{k\ge0}C_k|S_k|^*,
\qquad
C_k=
\int_{B_k}^{R(A_k)}u\frac{R(A_k)-u}{R(A_k)+u}\,du. \tag{13}
\]

For a simple exact uniform bound, note that

\[
R(\eta)=\sqrt{1+\eta^2}>\frac{1009}{1000}.
\]

Hence every ledger width exceeds \(369/1000\).  Also

\[
\frac{B_k}{R(A_k)+B_k}
>
\frac{A_k+1/2}{2A_k+3/2}
\ge\frac{32}{89}.
\]

Therefore

\[
C_k>\frac{32}{89}\frac{(369/1000)^2}{2}
=\frac{136161}{5562500}
>c_* \tag{14}
\]

for every \(k\ge0\).

## 6. Assembly

The low schedule and all high ledgers occupy pairwise-disjoint Borel radial
intervals.  Layer-cake integration, (11), and (13)--(14) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L^{2*}(E)
&\ge\operatorname{Cap}_2\\
&\ge\operatorname{LowPay}+\operatorname{HighPay}\\
&\ge c_*\left(|L|^*+\sum_{k\ge0}|S_k|^*\right)\\
&\ge c_*\pi.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using the classical rational bound \(\pi>333/106\),

\[
c_*\pi
=
\frac{371}{16650}\pi
>
\frac{371}{16650}\frac{333}{106}
=
\frac7{100}. \tag{15}
\]

Together with the height branch (4), this proves the theorem.
