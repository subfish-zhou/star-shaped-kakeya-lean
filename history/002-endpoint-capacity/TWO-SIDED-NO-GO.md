# No-go theorem for a universal two-sided sliver improvement

Date: 2026-08-03

## Verdict

The conjectural improvement obtained by replacing the one-sided sliver covering
constant `1+2*mu` by a smaller universal "two-sided" constant is false for the
full class of unit chords.

More precisely, if the two-sided formulation permits one radial-window side to
vanish, then its admissible class contains every one-sided rigid sliver family.
Consequently its sharp universal covering constant is no smaller than the
one-sided sharp value

\[
\boxed{1+2\mu}.
\]

The proposed constants `1` and `(1+2*mu)/(1+mu)` are therefore impossible.
The conditional figures `0.056852*pi` and `0.037902*pi` do not follow from this
mechanism.

## 1. Exact unit-chord embedding of a one-sided sliver

Work on the projective direction circle.  For a direction \(\alpha\), choose a
unit tangent vector \(u_\alpha\) and a perpendicular unit normal
\(n_\alpha\).  Given \(0\le h<r<R\), signs \(s,\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), define

\[
N_{\alpha,h,s,\varepsilon}
=
\{s h n_\alpha+t\varepsilon u_\alpha:0\le t\le1\}.
\]

This is a physical unit chord of projective direction \(\alpha\).  The
perpendicular foot from the origin is its endpoint

\[
P=s h n_\alpha,
\]

with radius \(A=h<r\).  The other endpoint has radius

\[
B=\sqrt{h^2+1}.
\]

Whenever \(R\le B\), the radial window \([r,R]\) intersects exactly the branch
\(t>0\):

\[
\sqrt{r^2-h^2}\le t\le\sqrt{R^2-h^2}.
\]

The opposite branch \(t<0\) is absent from the chord.  Thus the near-side
sliver is empty, while the far-side sliver is exactly

\[
\alpha-\sigma
\left[
\arcsin(h/R),\arcsin(h/r)
\right]
\]

for a freely selectable sign \(\sigma=-s\varepsilon\).  Both one-sided
orientations are realized by changing \(s\varepsilon\).

Changing the local lift
\((u_\alpha,n_\alpha)\mapsto(-u_\alpha,-n_\alpha)\), together with
\((s,\varepsilon)\mapsto(-s,-\varepsilon)\), leaves the physical chord
unchanged.  Hence the construction is intrinsic on the projective circle.

At \(h=0\), the window excludes the origin because \(r>0\); the same formula
has the correct projective limiting angle.  There is no undefined-argument
problem.

## 2. Sharp-constant consequence

Let `C1` be the one-sided rigid-sliver class and `C2` the purported two-sided
class allowing a side to vanish.  The construction above is a cost-preserving
embedding

\[
\mathcal C_1\hookrightarrow\mathcal C_2.
\]

The one-sided covering lemma has sharp constant \(1+2\mu\), approached by a
periodic microstructure.  Therefore every universal constant for `C2` obeys

\[
K_{\mathrm{two-sided}}\ge1+2\mu.
\]

In particular, for \(\mu>0\),

\[
\frac{1+2\mu}{1+\mu}<1+2\mu,
\]

so the claimed improved constant is refuted.

If a proposed theorem additionally requires both sides to be strictly positive,
the same obstruction persists by chords whose missing-side length tends to
zero, unless the theorem imposes a uniform quantitative lower bound excluding
this boundary.  Such an extra hypothesis is not available for arbitrary
star-shaped Kakeya sets.

## 3. Why the earlier analytic claim failed

The rejected derivation had two independent gaps.

1. Its component argument established only that a component can serve points
   up to distance `mu*length` on both sides, which is precisely the enlargement
   factor `1+2*mu`.  The asserted division by `1+mu` was introduced without a
   pairing or disjointness proof.
2. Its proposed periodic extremizer placed one selected sliver in the sparse
   union but did not place the second sliver generated by a genuinely centered
   chord in that union.  The numerical script likewise selected one side per
   direction.  That script is nevertheless a valid adversary for the universal
   two-sided claim because the foot-at-endpoint embedding realizes its selected
   side by a full physical unit chord whose other radial-window side vanishes.

The reported number

\[
\frac12-\frac23\log2
\]

is an algebraic value, but it was not the integral produced by the claimed
variable covering factor.  Therefore it is not a proved Kakeya lower bound.

## 4. Reproducible adversary

The checked-in script `two_sided_sliver_adversary.py` constructs explicit
periodic one-sided microstructures and measures finite unions of circular arcs.
The foot-at-endpoint embedding above turns each selected sliver into a genuine
unit-chord example.  For `R=1/2`, the computed densities converge to

\[
\frac1{1+2\mu}=\frac{R-r}{R+r}.
\]

## 5. Near-diameter rigidity does not by itself prove a factor two

Restricting to chords with \(M\) close to \(1/2\) excludes the
foot-at-endpoint family, but the naive proof still cannot simply add the two
one-sided covering estimates.  For each fixed chord the two radial-window
slivers may be disjoint; this does **not** imply that

\[
\bigcup_\theta W^+_\theta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\bigcup_\theta W^-_\theta
\]

are disjoint.  A rotated copy of a near-diameter chord can place part of its
positive sliver on the negative sliver of another chord while preserving
\(A,B,h,m,M\).  Thus the asserted exact global separation is false.

For calibration, rotate a fixed unit chord whose perpendicular foot is its
midpoint at distance \(h>0\).  If \(a=\arctan(2h)\), the two endpoint images are
half-circles shifted by \(\pi-2a\), so their overlap has length

\[
2a=2\arctan(2h)\longrightarrow0
\qquad(h\downarrow0),
\]

not an order-one limit.  This example invalidates the **disjoint-union proof
step**, but does not refute a more delicate near-diameter estimate with an
explicit overlap error.  In particular, an exact factor-two theorem for a
properly formulated restricted class remains unresolved; it would require a
global overlap bound, orientation/cut argument, or a different payment.

At the exact boundary \(h=0\), all needles pass through the origin and the
correct sharp result is the existing \(\pi/4\) theorem.  It is proved by the
paired endpoint identity, not by the unproved disjoint-union step.
